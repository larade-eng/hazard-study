Hazard Identification Study

A 15 minute task on how engineers identify ways a product can fail. Part of a graduate research project at the University of Central Florida.

What you will do

You will be shown a consumer medical device and asked to list the ways it can fail and the effect of each failure. The task is timed at 15 minutes. Some participants are also shown a set of AI generated suggestions during the task. Afterwards you answer four short questions.

What is recorded

Your worksheet entries, the time you take, and your answers to the closing questions. No name, email, or any other identifying information is collected, and nothing links your responses to you. Results are reported in aggregate in a course paper. Participation is voluntary and you may close the page at any point.

Before you begin

The task starts when you press the button at the bottom of this page and runs for 15 minutes. Set aside the time before you start, since the timer does not pause.

Device under analysis

Home automated blood pressure monitor. An over the counter device that adults use at home to measure systolic and diastolic blood pressure and pulse on the upper arm.

  1. Inflatable arm cuff, air tubing, electric air pump, pressure release valve.
  2. Pressure sensor and microcontroller running an oscillometric measurement algorithm.
  3. LCD display, four AA batteries.
  4. Bluetooth module that syncs readings to a phone app holding up to two user profiles.

Used unsupervised at home with no clinician present. Users include older adults, some of whom adjust their medication in response to readings. The phone app's own interface and the manufacturing process are out of scope.

Your task

List as many ways this device can fail as you can. For each one, give the effect on the user. Work as you would on a design FMEA. There is no required number of entries.
